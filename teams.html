Director's Reorganization Playbook

An interactive, chapter-by-chapter organizational audit for Engineering Directors redesigning teams. Based on the insights of Team Topologies: Organizing Business and Technology Teams for Fast Flow. Use this to guide your strategy when structuring your four teams.

Want to read the source material? Access teams_top.md.
Chapter 1

The Problem with Org Charts

Static org charts fail to represent the actual lines of communication and flow of value in a company. Rigid, hierarchical lines create operational silos and coordination bottlenecks that stall the flow of changes.
Director Reflections (Auditing 4 Teams)
Chapter 2

Conway's Law & Architectural Flow

Systems architecture matches the communication paths of the organization. To achieve a loosely coupled, scalable architecture, directors must intentionally design team communication structures (the Reverse Conway Maneuver).
Director Reflections (Auditing 4 Teams)
Chapter 3

Team-First Thinking & Cognitive Load

Teams should be small (5-9 people), stable, and long-lived. Directors must assess and limit team cognitive load (intrinsic, extraneous, germane) to keep the system manageable and prevent burnout.
Director Reflections (Auditing 4 Teams)
Core Elements of a Team API:
  • Code & Interfaces: Code repositories, APIs, library packages, and data schemas owned by the team.
  • Documentation: Up-to-date wikis, getting-started guides, runbooks, and design documentation.
  • User Personas & Scenarios: Clarity on who uses their services (e.g., developers, operations, end-users) and how they consume them.
  • Communication Protocols: Guidelines on how other teams should get in touch (e.g., Slack channels, email, office hours vs asynchronous updates).
  • Service Agreements & Support: Standard on-call rotations, SLA limits, ticket guidelines, and response-time expectations.
  • Future Roadmap: Current WIP, future milestones, and feature plans to give dependent teams early visibility.
Chapter 4

Static Team Topologies

Avoid chaotic organization charts by adopting clear, structured team templates optimized for fast flow. Siloed DevOps or SRE teams can act as anti-patterns if they introduce handoffs.
Director Reflections (Auditing 4 Teams)
Chapter 5

The Four Fundamental Team Topologies

Organize your engineering department using only four fundamental team types: Stream-Aligned, Enabling, Complicated-Subsystem, and Platform teams. Stream-aligned teams are the primary value delivery drivers.
Director Reflections (Auditing 4 Teams)
Chapter 6

Choose Team-First Boundaries

Divide software monoliths into manageable pieces using clear "fracture planes"—such as business domains, compliance boundaries, change cadence, or user personas.
Director Reflections (Auditing 4 Teams)
Chapter 7

Three Essential Team Interaction Modes

Teams must interact only via three well-defined modes: Collaboration (joint discovery), X-as-a-Service (consumption of a service), and Facilitating (helping/teaching).
Director Reflections (Auditing 4 Teams)
Chapter 8

Organizational Sensing & Evolution

Team topologies must continuously evolve in response to technological advances, strategic pivots, or operational growth. Reorgs should be continuous and sensing, not static five-year mandates.
Director Reflections (Auditing 4 Teams)
