Chapter 2
The Four Metrics That Actually Matter
Deployment Frequency
Proxy for batch size. High performers deploy on demand — multiple times per day. Low performers: once per month or less.
Lead Time for Changes
Time from code commit to production. High performers: <1 hour. Low performers: 1 month to 6 months.
Mean Time to Restore (MTTR)
How quickly you recover from incidents. High performers restore in under an hour. Low performers may take days.
Change Failure Rate
% of changes that degrade production. High performers: 0–15%. Low performers: 31–45%. Speed and stability are NOT a trade-off.
2×
High performers are twice as likely to exceed profitability, productivity, and market share goals. Don't measure lines of code, velocity, or utilization — measure outcomes.
Chapter 3
Culture is Measurable — and Changeable
Pathological
Power-oriented. Information hoarded. Messengers shot. Failure leads to scapegoating. Fear dominates.
🟡
Bureaucratic
Rule-oriented. Departments protect turf. Information neglected. Failure leads to justice. Novelty is a problem.
🟢
Generative
Performance-oriented. High cooperation, trust, and information flow. Failure leads to inquiry. Novelty implemented.
Behavior changes culture
"The way to change culture is not to first change how people think, but to start by changing how people behave." — John Shook, Lean Manufacturing.
Technical practices drive culture
Implementing Continuous Delivery and Lean management creates generative culture — not the other way around. Start with the practices.
Chapter 4
Continuous Delivery is Not Optional
Trunk-based development wins
Branches live <1 day. Fewer than 3 active branches. No code freezes. Long-lived branches inhibit refactoring and communication.
Tests owned by developers
Automated tests maintained by QA or outsourced parties are NOT correlated with performance. Developers must own test quality.
Config in version control
System and application configuration in version control correlates MORE strongly with performance than application code alone. Don't skip it.
Quality reduces unplanned work
High performers spend 49% on new work, 21% on unplanned work. Low performers: 38% new, 27% unplanned. Quality is a capacity investment.
75%
Work/life balance satisfaction at Microsoft Bing after implementing CD — up from 38% before. Technical investments are people investments.
Chapter 5
Architecture Determines Team Velocity
Loose coupling is the #1 lever
In 2017, loosely coupled architecture was the single biggest predictor of Continuous Delivery — larger than test automation or deployment automation.
Testability & deployability over tools
System type (mainframe, microservices, mobile) has NO significant correlation with performance. Architectural characteristics do. Focus on outcomes, not tech stacks.
Inverse Conway Maneuver
Organizations produce systems that mirror their communication structures. Design your team structure to achieve the desired architecture — not the reverse.
Let teams choose their tools
When teams choose their own tools, delivery performance improves. Architects should focus on enabling outcomes, not enforcing approved lists.
↑ linear
High-performing teams deploy at an increasing rate as they add developers. Low performers deploy less frequently as team size grows. Architecture drives this difference.
Chapter 6
Security Shifts Left — or Gets Left Behind
Security is a delivery capability
Building security into daily work — not as a downstream gate — improves delivery performance AND security outcomes simultaneously.
Infosec belongs in the team
Security reviews during design, feedback during demos, security tests in CI pipelines. 1 infosec person per 100 developers isn't enough for downstream reviews.
Preapproved libraries, not reviews
Shift from "infosec reviews your code" to "developers use approved libraries that make security the path of least resistance." Scale wins.
Late security is expensive
Security compliance as a downstream phase before go-live can add months to delivery and create large architectural rework. The earlier, the cheaper.
50%
High performers spend 50% less time remediating security issues than low performers. "Shift left" pays for itself in freed capacity.
Chapter 7
Lean Management Creates Flow — and Culture
WIP limits expose obstacles
WIP limits alone don't predict performance. They must be combined with visual dashboards and feedback loops to drive real process improvements.
Make work visible
Visual dashboards showing quality, defects, and productivity — accessible to teams and leaders alike — correlate strongly with delivery performance and culture.
CABs actively harm performance
External Change Advisory Boards are negatively correlated with lead time, deploy frequency, and MTTR — and have zero correlation with change fail rate. They are worse than no process at all.
Peer review works
Teams using peer review or no approval process achieve higher performance. Lightweight peer review + deployment pipeline satisfies regulatory requirements too.
CAB
"Approval by an external body simply doesn't work to increase the stability of production systems — it is, in fact, worse than having no change approval process at all."
Chapter 8
Lean Product Development Creates a Virtuous Cycle
Small batches unlock learning
Work decomposed into features deliverable in <1 week. MVPs validate business models. Small batches enable fast A/B testing and course correction.
Customer feedback must be active
Regularly collecting satisfaction metrics and incorporating them into design — not as an afterthought — predicts delivery and organizational performance.
Teams need authority to experiment
Development teams must be able to update specs and try new approaches without external approval. Inhibiting this inhibits innovation and degrades outcomes.
It's a virtuous cycle
Better delivery performance → better Lean product practices → better products → higher delivery performance. Invest in either side to accelerate both.
Faux Agile
Many orgs do "Agile" sprints while still spending months on requirements and releasing quarterly. That's waterfall with standups. True Lean product dev requires real team autonomy.
Chapter 9
Burnout is Preventable — and Your Problem to Fix
Deployment pain is a signal
"How painful are your deployments?" is a leading indicator of performance, culture, and burnout. If deploys require off-hours windows — you have an architecture problem.
6 burnout risk factors
Work overload · Lack of control · Insufficient rewards · Broken community · Absence of fairness · Value conflicts. All of these are management's responsibility to address.
Fix the environment, not the person
Organizations try to fix burned-out people — coaching, resilience training. Research is clear: fix the work environment. The system creates the outcome.
Values alignment reduces burnout
Mismatch between organizational values and individual values is a direct burnout predictor. The stated mission statement vs. lived reality gap matters enormously.
$300B
Annual US economic cost of job stress — sick time, long-term disability, and excessive turnover. Burnout prevention is also fiduciary responsibility, not just culture work.
Chapter 10
People Identity Drives Organizational Performance
eNPS predicts business outcomes
Employees in high-performing orgs are 2.2× more likely to recommend their company as a great place to work. Employee loyalty drives revenue growth.
Identity reduces burnout
When employees identify with the organization's values and goals, burnout decreases. CD and Lean practices build this identity by giving teams ownership of quality.
Tools & autonomy drive satisfaction
Job satisfaction depends on having the right tools, resources, and judgment in your work. Automation frees people for meaningful, decision-based work — that's what they value.
Diversity matters — it's absent
Diverse teams perform better. In 2017: 91% of respondents male, 33% worked on all-male teams. Diversity without inclusion doesn't hold. Fix both.
2.5×
Companies with highly engaged workers grew revenues 2.5× faster than those with low engagement. This is a P&L lever, not just an HR metric.
Chapter 11
Transformational Leaders Amplify — They Don't Replace — Good Practices
Vision
Clear understanding of where the team is going in 5 years. Without this, teams optimize locally at the expense of organizational goals.
Intellectual Stimulation
Challenges teams to question assumptions and think about old problems in new ways. Essential for technical innovation.
Supportive + Recognition
Considers personal needs. Praises achievement. Teams with the lowest transformational leadership scores are half as likely to be high performers.
Leaders alone don't deliver
Teams with the top 10% strongest transformational leaders are equally or less likely to be high performers. Leaders enable the practices — they don't replace them.
Invest in your people
Training budgets, conference attendance, hack days, 20% time, internal DevOps mini-conferences. These are not perks — they are organizational capability investments.
